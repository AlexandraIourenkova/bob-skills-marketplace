# Judging criteria and how to score against them

The criteria below are the challenge's own, four criteria with five points each, twenty in total, averaged over the judges. The hackathon site is the authority: when it differs from this file, the site wins, and the user should say so.

| Criterion | The judges ask |
|---|---|
| Completeness and feasibility | How feasible is the solution? How fully has the idea been thought out and planned? How complete is the proof of concept? How clear is the application of IBM technology? |
| Creativity and innovation | How unique and original is the approach in applying AI to the issue? Is the solution differentiated in the market? |
| Design and usability | How good is the design, the user experience and the ease of use? How quickly and easily could it be put to use in the real world and adopted by its users? |
| Effectiveness and efficiency | Does it address a high-priority, relevant issue? Does it achieve its goal effectively and efficiently? Can it achieve a measurable impact? Can it scale to more users or use cases? |

## Scoring an idea, 1 to 5

A score is the team's estimate for ranking ideas against each other. It predicts nothing about the judges; say so whenever you show scores. Give every score one line of reason, and score the idea as the team could deliver it in the time they have, not as it could be one day.

**Completeness and feasibility**

- 1: needs data, access or skills the team does not have; no path to a running slice.
- 3: a running slice is likely, with one open dependency (an access, an approval, an unknown interface).
- 5: one thin slice can run end to end in the first week on data the team can make; IBM technology has an obvious, necessary role.

**Creativity and innovation**

- 1: the first idea everyone has (a chat over documents, a summary of tickets) with nothing added.
- 3: a known pattern applied where it has not been applied, or a familiar problem attacked from an unusual side.
- 5: changes the shape of the work, not its speed; a reader says "I have not seen it done this way" and existing products do not do it.

**Design and usability**

- 1: only its builders can operate it; the demo needs explaining.
- 3: a real user can follow it with a short introduction.
- 5: the user is productive in the first minutes, in a place they already work; the demo needs no narration.

**Effectiveness and efficiency**

- 1: the problem is minor or the effect cannot be measured.
- 3: a real problem and a plausible effect, on assumed numbers.
- 5: a painful, frequent problem with a measured baseline, an effect the proof of concept can itself measure, and a clear next group of users.

## What usually loses points

- A plan with no running slice (first criterion), or a running slice nobody planned beyond.
- IBM technology that could be removed without anyone noticing.
- No answer to "what would people use instead": the option of doing nothing is always a competitor.
- A demo that shows the builder's view, not the user's.
- An impact stated without a baseline, or a baseline nobody measured.
