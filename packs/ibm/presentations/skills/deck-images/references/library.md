# The picture library

The pack ships 84 pictures in `assets/library`, generated by the pack authors from the prompts below, cropped to 16:9 and saved as JPEG of 20 to 300 KB: pack-a abstract vector, pack-b black and white photo art, pack-c dark technology, pack-d people at work, pack-e paper craft, pack-f desk objects, pack-g IBM-style isometric, pack-h IBM dark premium.

## Rights, in three lines

- An image a person generates with their own account is theirs to use under that generator's terms. There is no third-party licence to record and no attribution to carry, unlike a stock photo or a Creative Commons picture.
- The terms still matter: a few tools restrict commercial use on a free tier, and a few claim rights to the output. Check the tool once, then reuse the answer for the whole library.
- Independent of the tool: no real people and no likenesses, no company logos or trademarks, no imitation of a living artist's style, and no generated image presented as a photograph of a real event. Charts are drawn from data, never generated.

Record each generated image in `deck/images/credits.md` as "generated, <tool>, <date>, prompt in library.md" and write "AI-generated image" in the speaker notes of the slide that uses it. That is honesty, not a licence requirement.

## How the library is organised

Bundled: `assets/library/<pack>/<name>.jpg` inside the skill, with `manifest.json` (keywords, which themes each pack suits, which pictures work as covers) and `NOTICE`. Personal additions: `~/Pictures/deck-library/<pack>/<name>.png`, the same folder names, listed after the bundled set. Names describe the subject, not the deck: `growth`, `handover`, `security`. A deck links to a treated copy in its own `images/` folder, so the deck stays self-contained and the library is never modified.

Generate at 1920 x 1080 or larger for a full-bleed backdrop, 1200 px wide for a picture beside text; 1376 x 768, which some generators return for 16:9, is fine on a 1280 x 720 stage and on a projector, and only goes soft on a 4K screen or a large print. Keep the raw output; crop and tint per deck with `visual_assets.py treat`, which is what makes pictures from different sessions look like one set.

## Or no picture at all

A deck does not have to use a photograph. `visual_assets.py backdrop` draws the title and section backgrounds from the theme's own colours: `grid` for a strict engineering structure, `bands` for diagonal blocks, `contours` and `waves` for calm movement, `mesh` for a soft gradient field, plus `dots`, `noise`, `shapes` and `arcs`. SVG of a few kilobytes for the HTML deck, PNG of the same name for the .pptx, no licence, no credit, and colours that cannot clash. The style preview shows these beside the library packs, so the user picks between a photograph and a drawn background in the same breath. Use a different seed and `--intensity 0.55` for the section backdrop, so it is not the title slide again.

## Text on the left

Every prompt in this file puts the subject on the right and leaves the left third empty, and the decks rely on that: titles over a picture sit on the left, a `split` slide puts the picture on the right half. Keep that when adding pictures: a new picture with its subject on the left breaks every title slide it lands on. When a generator ignores the instruction, mirror the picture horizontally before saving it, unless it shows something that must not be mirrored (a clock face, a keyboard, readable objects).

## One picture, one place

- The title picture is the deck's face: used once, on the title slide, and nowhere else.
- Sections: either one neutral picture repeated on every divider (the manifest marks the neutral ones per pack), or a different picture per chapter, given by a picture line under the `# Chapter` heading. Never the title picture.
- Content slides: each picture once. A deck that shows the same picture three times looks like it ran out of ideas.

## Using them in a deck

1. Find it: `visual_assets.py library --find growth` lists the packs and pictures with sizes and paths.
2. Crop and unify straight from the library: `visual_assets.py treat ~/Pictures/deck-library/pack-a/growth.png --theme deck/theme.json --crop 16:9 --width 1920 -o images/growth.jpg`, adding `--duotone` when the picture's colours are not the theme's, and `--scrim bottom` for a title backdrop that carries a headline. Write JPEG: a generated PNG of 1.5 MB becomes about 200 KB at quality 84, and the HTML deck embeds every picture, so the difference is the difference between a 1 MB file and a 10 MB one. Keep PNG only for a flat vector picture with few colours that must stay perfectly crisp.
3. Reference it: `backdrop: images/growth.png` in the front matter for the title and section slides, or `![Growth as stepped blocks](images/growth.png)` on a slide of its own.
4. Alt text says what it shows and why it is there. A backdrop that carries no meaning gets "decorative background".

## Growing the library

Ten images per pack is enough for a year of decks. New pictures go to `~/Pictures/deck-library/<pack>` first and appear in `library` at once; the pack maintainers then fold them into `assets/library` as JPEG, with metadata stripped and a re-encode whenever the compressed bytes happen to contain a sequence the scanners read as hidden Unicode. Add one when a deck needs a picture the library does not have, generate it with the pack's style block so it matches, and keep it. Do not mix packs inside one deck: an abstract vector picture next to a monochrome photograph looks like two decks glued together.
