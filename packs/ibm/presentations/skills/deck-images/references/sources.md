# Image sources

This file lists where to get photographs, illustrations, icons, backgrounds and maps that are safe to use in a company deck, what each licence allows, and how to record credits. Read it when a storyboard calls for a picture and before you give the user search terms, then again when the files arrive in `images/` and you write the credits. The agent never downloads pictures. It names the source and the search terms, the user picks and saves the files, and the agent records who made each picture and under which licence.

## Licence cheat sheet

| Licence | Commercial deck | Credit required | Modifications | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CC0, public domain | Yes | No, but record the source anyway | Yes | Covers copyright only. Trademarks, logos and the privacy of people in the picture are separate rights. A "public domain mark" is a statement about copyright status, not a grant, and status can differ by country. |
| CC BY (4.0, 3.0, 2.0) | Yes | Yes: title, author, source, licence | Yes, and say what you changed | The credit travels with the picture wherever the deck goes. Older versions word the credit slightly differently; the same four facts cover all of them. |
| CC BY-SA | Yes | Yes | Yes, but an adapted picture must carry the same licence | A duotone, a tint or a heavy crop makes an adaptation, which then has to be shared under BY-SA. Whether the deck itself is affected is legally unclear. Prefer another picture. If you must, use it unmodified in a contained box and credit it. |
| CC BY-NC | No | | | NonCommercial is about the use, not the user. A company presentation, internal or external, counts as commercial in most readings. Do not use. |
| CC BY-ND | Yes, unmodified only | Yes | No: no crop, no tint, no duotone | The full-bleed cover crop counts as a change. Place it in a contained box at its own ratio, or pick another picture. |
| Unsplash licence | Yes | No, appreciated | Yes | Unsplash+ pictures are paid and separately licensed; filter them out. The licence does not cover logos, brands or people shown in the picture. |
| Pexels licence | Yes | No, appreciated | Yes | Identifiable people and brands must not appear to endorse anything. No selling of unaltered copies. Sponsored stock results appear in searches; skip them. |
| Pixabay Content Licence | Yes | No, appreciated | Yes | Sponsored results sit above the free ones. Many uploads are AI-generated and labelled as such. People shown must not be presented in a misleading or offensive way. |
| MIT, ISC, Apache-2.0 (icon sets) | Yes | No credit on the slide. Keep the licence text with the files if you redistribute the set | Yes | Apache-2.0 asks you to keep the NOTICE file when redistributing the software. Placing icons in a deck is not redistribution of the set. |
| SIL OFL 1.1 (fonts) | Yes, embedding included | No | Yes, under a different name | Fonts cannot be sold on their own. A modified font must drop the reserved font name. |
| "Free for personal use" | No | | | Not a licence for company work. Common on font and illustration sites. Buy the commercial licence or move on. |
| Rights-managed stock | Only with a purchased licence | As the contract says | As the contract says | Licensed per use, territory, duration and medium. A deck that goes from internal to external may need a second licence. Keep the invoice next to the credits. |
| Editorial use only | No | | | Reserved for news and commentary. A business deck does not qualify, even when the picture is free. |

When the search results show one licence and the file page shows another, the file page wins. When the file page is ambiguous, check the licence page of the site, and if it is still unclear, pick another picture.

### Pictures with people, logos and artworks

- A licence covers the photographer's copyright. It does not cover the rights of the people, brands and artworks in the frame.
- Identifiable people: use for illustration only. Never imply that they use, endorse or work for anything. A model release is what allows commercial use of a face; free sites rarely have one. For employees, get written consent and keep it with the credits.
- Logos and trademarks in the background of a photo are usually tolerated as incidental. A logo as the subject of the picture is not; crop it out or choose another picture.
- Artworks, sculptures and some buildings carry their own rights even when the photo is free. Museum open-access sets solve this for old works; for modern art and architecture, check the licence page of the photo and the site.
- Personal data in a photo (name badges, screens, documents, licence plates) is a privacy problem before it is a copyright problem. Blur or crop.

## Maps and data

| Source | Host | What | Licence | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Natural Earth | naturalearthdata.com | Country, coastline, river and city vectors at three scales | Public domain | Not required |
| OpenStreetMap | openstreetmap.org | Street-level map data and rendered tiles | ODbL for the data | Required: "© OpenStreetMap contributors". The public tile servers have a usage policy; render your own or use a tile provider for anything beyond a single screenshot. |
| Blank maps on Wikimedia Commons | commons.wikimedia.org | Simplified world and country outlines as SVG | Per file, often public domain or CC BY-SA | Per file |

Use a map only when geography is the point: where the customers are, which regions go live in which quarter, where the plants stand. A map as a symbol of "global reach" says nothing; a statement slide says it better. Borders are political. If the company has an approved map, use it. Otherwise use Natural Earth and be aware that disputed boundaries follow its de facto view.

## Screenshots, product images and logos

1. Your own product: screenshots are yours to use. Blur or replace personal data, use demo accounts, and crop to the region that matters (see treatment.md).
2. Third-party products: the vendor holds the copyright on the interface. A plain screenshot for factual comparison in a company deck is usually tolerated, but it is not licensed. Check the vendor's brand or screenshot guidelines, never alter a screenshot to misrepresent the product, and do not use a competitor's screenshot to disparage them.
3. Company logos: trademarks, not copyright. Use your own logo from the brand portal, and partner or customer logos only with their permission and from their brand page. Do not recolour, stretch or crop a logo. A wall of customer logos implies endorsement; confirm each one is cleared.
4. The company brand portal is the first stop for logos, photos, product renders and templates. Everything there is already cleared for company use. Ask the user for the portal path or file drop before searching public sources.
5. Stock product renders (a generic laptop, a generic phone) come from the same sources as photos. Avoid renders that show a competitor's product or a recognisable brand design.

## How to search well

| Do | Instead of |
|---|---|
| Search with concept plus setting plus mood: "engineer inspecting turbine, wind farm, overcast, wide" | "business" |
| Set the orientation to landscape; the slide is 16:9 and a portrait wastes half of it | Cropping a portrait to a strip |
| Filter by the theme's accent colour, or by a neutral tone that the treatment will unify | Mixing warm and cool pictures in one deck |
| Take three pictures from one photographer, collection or shoot | One picture from each of three sites |
| Look for people doing something, not looking at the camera, with a natural mix of ages, genders and backgrounds | Posed teams, stacked hands, thumbs up |
| Pick pictures with quiet areas where text can sit | Busy, centred subjects with no room for the headline |
| Skip handshakes, pointing at whiteboards, rocket ships, light bulbs, chess pieces, puzzle pieces, arrows going up | Any of those |
| Prefer the muted, slightly under-exposed picture; the treatment adds what it needs | The saturated one that will clash with the accent |

Give the user three to five search terms per picture, the source to search on, the orientation, and what to avoid. Ask them to save into `images/` under the name from the storyboard and to paste the picture's page title and author, or the page address, into the chat so the credit can be written.

## Credits

Every deck with third-party pictures has `images/credits.md`. One row per file, written as soon as the file arrives.

```markdown
| File | Title | Author | Source | Licence | Link (in words) | Modifications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| images/03-turbine.jpg | Wind turbine at dusk | Jane Doe | Unsplash | Unsplash licence | unsplash.com, photo ID abc123 | cropped to 16:9, duotone |
| images/07-harbour.jpg | Rotterdam harbour, 1962 | Nationaal Archief | Wikimedia Commons | CC0 | commons.wikimedia.org, file "Rotterdam haven 1962.jpg" | cropped |
| images/12-backdrop.png | Generated backdrop | visual_assets.py | This deck | Company | generated, mesh, theme colours | none |
```

The link is written in words: the host and an identifier, not a pasted address. That survives copy and paste into notes and slides and stays readable when the page moves.

Where the credit goes in the deck:

| Licence | Where |
|---|---|
| CC0, public domain, Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay | `Notes:` line of the slide ("Photo: Jane Doe via Unsplash"). Nothing on the slide. |
| CC BY, Font Awesome icons, Hero Patterns | A `Source:` line on the slide, or a credits slide at the end: `## Image credits` with one bullet per picture. Use the credits slide when there are more than two. |
| Museum and archive items | `Notes:` line naming the institution; a caption when the object itself is discussed. |
| Editorial pictures where the picture is the content | A caption paragraph on the image slide: who, what, when. |
| Generated images | `Notes:` line "AI-generated image" and the prompt in `images/credits.md` (see prompts.md). |

Attribution line for CC BY: "Title" by Author, CC BY 4.0, via Source. If you changed the picture, say so: "Title" by Author, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons, cropped and tinted. Keep the four facts (title, author, source, licence) even when the licence version differs.

## Decision list

1. The company brand portal: cleared photos, logos, renders, templates. Ask for it first.
2. CC0 and public domain: museums, archives, government galleries, Open Peeps, Open Doodles. No credit needed; record anyway.
3. Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay: broad choice, no credit needed, watch for people and brands.
4. CC BY with credits: Wikimedia Commons, Openverse, Humaaans, Storyset, Hero Patterns. Write the credit before placing the picture.
5. Generated images with the prompts in prompts.md, when the company allows them and the subject is abstract.
6. Never "found on the web": image search results, social media, other companies' decks, news sites, or anything with no licence page. If you cannot name the licence, you cannot use the picture.
