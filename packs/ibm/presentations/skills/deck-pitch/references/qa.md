# Questions and answers

This file tells you how to predict the questions a deck will get, prepare answers backed by evidence from the storyboard, and coach the speaker on handling the room. Read it after the key messages and the talk track exist (see `pitch.md`) and before you fill the "Q&A bank" and "Backup slides" sections of `talk-kit.md`. Every answer points at a slide in the main deck or at a backup slide in the appendix; an answer with no slide behind it is a gap in the storyboard, and you fix the storyboard before you write the answer. The examples use the sample deck from `pitch.md`: two enterprise onboarding hires, 240 K, decision by 30 September.

## 1. Build the question bank from the storyboard

Go through the storyboard slide by slide. Every number, claim, comparison, chart, timeline and ask invites questions. Generate them with the prompts below, write each as the audience would say it, and record the slide it came from. A 12-slide deck produces 40 to 60 questions; that is the bank the rest of this file ranks and answers.

| Slide contains | Prompts to generate questions |
|---|---|
| A percentage or a ratio | What is the base of this percentage? How many cases is that? Over what period? What was it a year ago? |
| An absolute number | Where does it come from? Who else has this number? Is it gross or net? What did you exclude? |
| A comparison or a chart | Are the two groups comparable? What else differs between them? Why this cut of the data? Would a different cut show the same? |
| A claim of cause | How do you know this causes that? What else could explain it? Who else did you ask? |
| A cost | Is it fully loaded? What is not in it? What does it become in year two? What would half the budget buy? |
| A forecast or a target | What assumptions drive it? What is the range? When will we know it is on track? |
| A timeline | What is the critical path? What has slipped before? What happens if the first date moves? |
| Alternatives considered | Why did this one lose? Who evaluated them? What would make you change the recommendation? |
| Risks | What is the worst case? Who owns each risk? What is the exit if it fails? |
| The ask | What happens if we do nothing? Why now? Who is accountable? What do you need from us, exactly? |
| Anything missing | Why is there no slide about X? What are you not showing us? |

Add the questions from the audience tables in section 2 that fit this audience, and the three questions the speaker fears most (ask the user for them).

## 2. Questions by audience

Each table lists the questions that audience asks most, and what to answer with. Prepare the ones that match the deck; add the others to the bank in case a mixed room brings them.

### Executives and boards

| Question | Answer with |
|---|---|
| What does it cost in total, including what is not on the slide? | the fully loaded figure, year two, and the cost slide |
| What is the risk if we do this and it does not work? | the risk slide, the review point, and the exit |
| What alternatives did you consider and why did you reject them? | the alternatives slide with costs and arrival dates |
| How long until we see a result, and how will we see it? | the timeline, the metric and where it is reported |
| Why now and not at the annual plan? | the cost of waiting in money per month |
| Who is accountable, and for what number by what date? | one name, one metric, one date |
| What would you do with half the budget? | the trade-off, stated in the same metric |
| What is the weakest number in this deck? | name it yourself, with the base and the caveat |
| Has Finance signed off the numbers? | the `Source:` lines and the name in Finance |
| How does this fit the priorities we agreed? | the objective it serves, by its official name |

### Investors

| Question | Answer with |
|---|---|
| How big is the market and how did you size it? | bottom-up: customers times price, then the top-down cross-check |
| What stops a larger competitor from copying this? | the moat slide: data, distribution, switching cost or speed |
| What are the unit economics: acquisition cost, payback, gross margin? | one table, per customer, with the trend |
| What is churn and why do customers leave? | the churn number, the cohort, the top two reasons |
| Why is this team the one to do it? | the team slide: what each person has already built |
| What exactly will the money buy and which milestone does it reach? | use of funds by line, and the milestone with a date |
| Why now? | the change in the market or the technology that opened the window |
| What is the month-on-month growth and what drives it? | the traction chart and the driver behind it |
| What is the biggest risk to the plan? | name it and the mitigation; never say "none" |
| Who else is in the round? | the facts, or "I cannot share that yet" |

### Customers and prospects

| Question | Answer with |
|---|---|
| What does it cost, all in, over three years? | licence, implementation, support, in one figure |
| How does it integrate with what we already run? | the integration slide, named systems, and a reference that did it |
| What support do we get, at what hours, and how fast? | the service levels, in numbers |
| Who like us uses this, and can we talk to them? | two references by name, with permission checked first |
| Where is our data, who can see it, and how is it protected? | the security slide and the certifications by name |
| What is committed on the roadmap for the next twelve months? | committed items only; label the rest as planned |
| What happens when we want to leave? | export format, notice period, data deletion |
| How long does implementation take and what do you need from us? | the timeline and the customer's tasks, by role |
| What happens when it fails? | the incident process, the service credits, a real example |
| Why you and not the competitor we saw last week? | two differences that matter to them, no criticism of the competitor |

### Technical peers

| Question | Answer with |
|---|---|
| How does it scale, and where is the first bottleneck? | the load test numbers and the component that limits them |
| What are the failure modes and what happens to in-flight data? | the failure table: mode, detection, recovery, data effect |
| Where does the data come from and how is it validated? | the data lineage slide and the validation rules |
| How is it secured: authentication, secrets, audit? | the security slide and the review that was done |
| Why not X? | the alternatives slide; X must be on it with the reason it lost |
| What is the migration path and the rollback? | the timeline with the rollback point marked |
| What did you measure and under what load? | the methodology backup slide |
| What does it cost to run: on-call, monitoring, upgrades? | the operational cost line and who carries it |
| Which parts are new and which are proven? | the architecture slide, coloured by maturity |
| What is still open? | the open questions slide, with owners and dates |

### All-hands and teams

| Question | Answer with |
|---|---|
| What changes for me next week? | the concrete changes by role, or "nothing until date X" |
| When does this start and when is it done? | the timeline in months |
| Does this affect jobs or reporting lines? | a direct yes or no, then the detail; never a vague answer |
| Who do I go to with questions? | one name and one channel |
| Why this option and what else was considered? | the alternatives slide in one sentence each |
| What happens to the project I am on now? | the list of affected projects, or where it will be published |
| How will we know it worked? | the metric and where everyone can see it |
| What do you need from us? | the ask, per team, in one line |

### Skeptics and hostile questioners

| Question | Answer with |
|---|---|
| This failed last time; why would it work now? | what is different, in two specifics, and what was learned |
| Who approved this, and when? | the names and dates, or the approval you are asking for today |
| Is this not just (a loaded label)? | restate the premise in neutral words, then the evidence |
| How much of this growth is real and not accounting? | the definition used and the `Source:` line |
| Other teams are cutting; why should you be different? | the return, in the same currency as the cuts |
| Were you not the person who ran the last onboarding project? | yes, and what that project taught; no defence of it |
| Why should we trust these numbers? | the source, the method on the backup slide, the person who checked them |
| What happens if we just say no? | the cost of inaction, from the deck, in one number |

### Regulators and auditors

| Question | Answer with |
|---|---|
| What evidence supports this figure and where is it kept? | the source system, the report name, the retention location |
| Who owns this control and who reviews it? | the owner by role and the review cadence |
| Show the trail from the source data to the slide. | the methodology backup slide, step by step |
| What are the exceptions and how were they handled? | the exception count, the log, the approver |
| When was this last tested and by whom? | the date, the tester, the result |
| Which policy or standard does this map to? | the clause, quoted by number |
| What has changed since the last review? | the change list with dates |
| Who has access and how is it removed? | the access list owner and the removal process |

## 3. The ten hardest questions

1. Score every question in the bank: damage if unanswered (1 low, 2 embarrassing, 3 kills the ask) times likelihood (1 unlikely, 2 possible, 3 certain). Nine is the top score.
2. Rank by score. Break ties in favour of questions about the ask and the cost.
3. Write the answers for the top ten in full, using the entry template in section 8. The rest of the bank keeps a one-line answer and a slide number.
4. Map each of the ten to a backup slide. If two answers need the same backup, one slide serves both. If an answer needs a backup that does not exist, add it to the storyboard's appendix now.
5. Give the speaker the ten to rehearse aloud in the third run (see `handouts.md`).

Scoring for the sample deck, the top six:

| Question | Damage | Likelihood | Score | Backup |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Why not wait until the annual plan in January? | 3 | 3 | 9 | A5 timeline, cost of waiting |
| Is a churn comparison on 84 accounts meaningful? | 3 | 2 | 6 | A1 methodology |
| Why not contractors until the automation lands? | 2 | 3 | 6 | A3 alternatives with costs |
| What if growth slows and we do not need them? | 2 | 3 | 6 | A4 risk register, exit |
| Who owns the outcome, and what if it misses? | 3 | 2 | 6 | A5 timeline with owner and review date |
| Is 240 K fully loaded, and what is year two? | 2 | 2 | 4 | slide 9 and A3 cost table |

## 4. Answer frameworks

- A, R, E: Answer in one sentence. Reason in one sentence. Example or Evidence in one sentence, pointing at a slide. Then stop. Silence after the evidence is not a gap; it is the end of the answer.
- Bridging: when the question is narrow, answer it, then connect to the message. "Yes, the cohort is 84 accounts. It is every enterprise account signed since last September, and it points the same way as the queue on slide 6: waiting costs accounts." Bridge after answering, never instead of answering.
- Not known yet: "I do not know. What I do know is X. I will have the answer by Thursday and send it to this group by email." Say all three parts. Write the commitment in the parking lot so the follow-up email carries it.
- Reframing a loaded question: restate the premise in neutral words and answer that. "The question is whether the growth is real. It is recognised revenue from the Finance close, not bookings. Slide 3." Do not repeat the loaded words.
- The parking lot: for off-topic questions, "that is a real question and it is not in this deck; I am writing it down and will come back to you after the meeting". Keep a visible list. Every item goes into the follow-up email.
- Answer the question that was asked. If the question was "how many accounts", the answer starts with a number, not with context. Give the context after the number if it is needed.
- Time-boxing: 60 seconds per answer. If the full answer takes longer, give the one-sentence version and offer the backup slide or a conversation after.
- When a question reveals a gap in the deck: say so. "That is a fair point and the deck does not cover it. Here is what I know today, and I will add it to the version I send tomorrow." Then log it as a storyboard fix.

## 5. Handling the room

1. Repeat or rephrase the question before answering. It buys three seconds, it checks the meaning, and the back of the room hears it.
2. Answer to the whole room, not only to the person who asked. Return your eyes to them for the last sentence.
3. Keep answers under 60 seconds. Watch for the moment the questioner nods; stop there.
4. Do not argue. Agree on what is true in the objection first ("the cohort is small, that is right"), then add what the objection misses.
5. Offer to continue after the meeting when an exchange passes two rounds. The room's time belongs to the room.
6. Remote Q&A: ask a moderator to read questions from the chat in the order received, or read them yourself at fixed points (after each section, not after each slide). Say the questioner's name and the question aloud before answering. Answer chat questions in the chat as well, in one line, so the record is complete.

## 6. Difficult moments

| Moment | Do this |
|---|---|
| Hostile tone | Lower your own pace and volume. Answer the content, not the tone. One answer, then move to the next question. |
| Interruption mid-talk | Answer if it takes one sentence. Otherwise: "Good question, it is on slide 9; two minutes." Keep the promise. |
| A senior person takes over | Let them finish. Thank them, take what supports your message, and return to the slide with the next headline. Never compete for the room. |
| A number you cannot recall | "The exact figure is on the source line of slide 5 and in the full table on A2." Jump there. Guessing a number costs more than the pause. |
| A competitor comparison | State the two differences that matter to this audience. Do not describe the competitor's weaknesses; the room checks them later. |
| A request to commit on the spot | Commit only to what is already on the ask slide. For anything else: "I can answer that by Friday with the numbers behind it." |
| A question already answered | Answer again in one sentence, without "as I said". |
| Silence when you ask for questions | Wait five seconds. Then ask the first question yourself: "The question I would ask is why not contractors. Here is why." |

## 7. Backup slides

Backup slides live in the appendix after a section slide named "Appendix". Prepare these for a decision deck:

| Backup | Content | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| A1 Methodology | how each headline number was measured, the definitions, the cohort, the exclusions | "how do you know", "is it meaningful" |
| A2 Full data table | every row behind a chart or a top-five table | "which accounts", "show me the detail" |
| A3 Alternatives with costs | each alternative, its cost, its arrival date, why it lost | "why not X" |
| A4 Risks and mitigations | the register: risk, likelihood, impact, owner, mitigation, trigger | "what could go wrong", "what is the exit" |
| A5 Timeline detail | week-level plan with owners and the review date | "when", "who", "why now" |
| A6 Glossary | every term the room may not share, one line each | "what do you mean by" |

Naming: the headline of a backup slide starts with its label, "A3. Alternatives considered, with costs", so the speaker can say "slide A3" and the audience can find it in the reading version. Record the physical slide number of each backup in the talk kit: in a PowerPoint slideshow the speaker jumps by typing the physical number and pressing Enter (A3 in the sample deck is slide 16). Press B for a black screen while talking without a slide; press the number again to return. In Presenter View, "See all slides" shows thumbnails for the same jump. Backup slides are not hidden slides: hidden slides do not print in the reading version and are easy to leave behind by mistake.

## 8. Q&A entry template and five examples

Template, one entry per question:

```
Question: <as the audience would say it>
Who asks: <role>
Why they ask: <the concern behind the question>
Answer: <three sentences: answer, reason, evidence>
Evidence: <the number and its Source: line>
Backup slide: <label and physical number, or "main deck, slide N">
```

Filled for the sample deck:

```
Question: Why not wait until the annual plan in January?
Who asks: the CFO
Why they ask: headcount outside the cycle sets a precedent
Answer: Because waiting has a price we can count. The queue grows by three accounts a month and the contracts signed this quarter hit their 90-day mark before January. Waiting one quarter puts about 30 accounts, 1.8 million of ARR, through the late path; at the six-point churn gap that is about 110 K of ARR lost, against 60 K of salary for the same quarter.
Evidence: 9 percent versus 3 percent churn, 84-account cohort; Source: CRM onboarding report, September 2026
Backup slide: A5 Timeline with cost of waiting, slide 18
```

```
Question: Is a churn comparison on 84 accounts statistically meaningful?
Who asks: the head of analytics
Why they ask: small cohorts have produced wrong decisions before
Answer: Eighty-four is every enterprise account signed in twelve months, so it is the population, not a sample. The gap is six percentage points and it holds in both halves of the year. If it were half as large, the hires would still pay back in year one.
Evidence: cohort split at 30 days; churn 9 percent (5 of 53) versus 3 percent (1 of 31); Source: CRM onboarding report
Backup slide: A1 Methodology, slide 14
```

```
Question: Why not contractors until the automation lands?
Who asks: the COO
Why they ask: contractors avoid a permanent cost
Answer: Contractors cost 310 K for the same hours and spend their first two months learning the product. The automation covers mid-market self-serve and lands in Q2; enterprise onboarding is integration work with the customer's IT team and stays manual. The two hires are the cheapest option that arrives in time.
Evidence: contractor quote 310 K, agency rate card August 2026; automation plan, Q2 milestone
Backup slide: A3 Alternatives with costs, slide 16
```

```
Question: What if growth slows and we do not need them?
Who asks: a board member
Why they ask: fixed cost added at the top of a growth curve
Answer: The roles are reviewed at six months against the time-to-first-value metric. If the queue is under ten accounts and the metric is under 25 days, one role moves to the mid-market team where the automation needs a product owner. Both hires carry a standard notice period; the exposure is one quarter of cost.
Evidence: capacity model, 22 accounts per person per quarter; review date 15 May
Backup slide: A4 Risk register, slide 17
```

```
Question: Who owns the outcome, and what happens if it misses?
Who asks: the CEO
Why they ask: an ask without an owner is a wish
Answer: I own it. The measure is time to first value under 25 days by 31 January, reported weekly on the customer success dashboard. If it is above 30 days at the six-month review, I bring the analysis and the options back to this group.
Evidence: dashboard metric, weekly; review date 15 May
Backup slide: A5 Timeline with owner and review date, slide 18
```
