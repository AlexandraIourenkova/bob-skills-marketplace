# Audiences, goals, formats and material

This file adapts a deck to the room: who listens, why they are there, how the deck is delivered and what material it starts from. Read it before choosing a structure in narratives.md, and again just before writing the storyboard, to run the checklist at the end. Every table gives defaults; a user who knows the audience overrides them.

## Audience profiles

Ask who is in the room and who decides. When the user names a role, use the matching profile. When the user is unsure, ask for one name and one job title and profile that person.

### Executives and boards

| Aspect | Default |
|---|---|
| Care about | The decision, the money, the risk, what happens if they say no |
| Attention span | The first slide and the last; expect interruptions from slide two |
| Opening | The answer, with the cost and the date |
| Evidence they trust | Their own numbers (finance close, board metrics), a pilot, a peer company |
| Depth | One level: the claim and its main number; detail in the appendix |
| Tone | Direct, short sentences, no hedging; say what you recommend |
| Switch off at | Background, history, method, more than one chart per slide |
| Ask they can grant | Budget, headcount, a go or no-go, a priority call between projects |

Structure: Pyramid, or Options and a recommendation. Ten slides, and the ask on the first content slide.

### Investors

| Aspect | Default |
|---|---|
| Care about | Market size, growth rate, unit economics, the team, what the money buys |
| Attention span | Three minutes for the story; the rest is questions |
| Opening | The traction number, or the business in one sentence |
| Evidence they trust | Revenue, retention, cohort data, named customers, comparables |
| Depth | Every claim has a number; every number has a source they can check |
| Tone | Confident, precise, no adjective without a number |
| Switch off at | Feature lists, vision without traction, a market size with no bottom-up path |
| Ask they can grant | An amount, a term, a next meeting |

Structure: Problem, Agitate, Solve for the story, inside the fixed pitch order (problem, solution, traction, market, model, team, ask) from the deck-pitch skill.

### Customers and prospects

| Aspect | Default |
|---|---|
| Care about | Their problem, their cost, their risk; not your product |
| Attention span | As long as the deck is about them |
| Opening | Their pain in their words, or a peer customer's result |
| Evidence they trust | Reference customers in their industry, a demo, a pilot offer, a price |
| Depth | Outcome first, mechanism on request, architecture in the appendix |
| Tone | Plain, their vocabulary for their process, no jargon they do not use |
| Switch off at | Company history, the org chart, values slides, a feature tour |
| Ask they can grant | A pilot, an introduction to the decider, a next meeting, a signature |

Structure: Problem, Agitate, Solve; Hero's journey when a reference customer carries the story; Demo tour when the product is the proof.

### Technical peers and engineers

| Aspect | Default |
|---|---|
| Care about | Whether it works, what it costs to run, what breaks, what they must change |
| Attention span | Long when the content is precise; lost at the first vague claim |
| Opening | The constraint or the failure that forces the design |
| Evidence they trust | Measurements, code, logs, a prototype, a benchmark with its method |
| Depth | Two levels: the design and its trade-offs; sources in the appendix |
| Tone | Exact terms, units, versions; say what you do not know |
| Switch off at | Marketing language, a diagram with no data flow, a benchmark with no setup |
| Ask they can grant | Agreement on a design, a review sign-off, effort estimates, an owner |

Structure: SCQA for a design proposal; Options and a recommendation for an architecture review, with the comparison table as the centre slide.

### The whole company and teams

| Aspect | Default |
|---|---|
| Care about | What changes for them, whether the company is doing well, what leaders decided |
| Attention span | Ten minutes; less on a video call |
| Opening | A win with a name attached, or the one number for the quarter |
| Evidence they trust | Customer stories, numbers they recognise, a colleague speaking |
| Depth | Shallow and concrete: one chart, one story, one change |
| Tone | Warm, plain, honest about bad news |
| Switch off at | Financial detail, strategy vocabulary, anything not about them |
| Ask they can grant | Attention, a change in behaviour, questions, volunteers |

Structure: Sparkline for the quarter's story; What, So what, Now what for a change announcement.

### Conference and public audiences

| Aspect | Default |
|---|---|
| Care about | Learning one thing they can use, being entertained, the speaker's credibility |
| Attention span | Twenty minutes after a strong open; phones appear at the first bullet wall |
| Opening | A story or a striking number in the first thirty seconds |
| Evidence they trust | A live demo, a failure told honestly, numbers from real use |
| Depth | One idea, three examples; the paper or the repository for the rest |
| Tone | Personal, concrete, with a point of view |
| Switch off at | Company introductions, product pitches, an agenda slide |
| Ask they can grant | Try the thing, read the post, follow up; nothing more |

Structure: Sparkline or Hero's journey; one idea per talk.

### Students and trainees

| Aspect | Default |
|---|---|
| Care about | Understanding, passing, doing the task afterwards |
| Attention span | Fifteen minutes per block, then an exercise |
| Opening | The question they will be able to answer at the end |
| Evidence they trust | Worked examples, a demonstration, a mistake and its fix |
| Depth | Full, in order: concept, example, exercise, summary |
| Tone | Patient, consistent vocabulary, one new term per slide |
| Switch off at | Twenty minutes without doing anything, undefined terms, skipped steps |
| Ask they can grant | Do the exercise, ask questions, use it next week |

Structure: Three acts per module (concept, practice, review); each block ends with a summary slide.

### Partners

| Aspect | Default |
|---|---|
| Care about | Their margin, their customers, what they must invest, what you commit to |
| Attention span | Medium; they came to negotiate |
| Opening | The joint opportunity in their numbers |
| Evidence they trust | Joint pipeline, a shared customer, a written commitment |
| Depth | Commercial terms explicit; product detail on request |
| Tone | Mutual, precise about who does what |
| Switch off at | One-sided plans, "synergies" without numbers |
| Ask they can grant | A joint plan, named contacts, a first shared customer |

Structure: Before, After, Bridge, with the joint plan as the bridge.

### Regulators, auditors and compliance

| Aspect | Default |
|---|---|
| Care about | Compliance with the rule, evidence, controls, who is accountable |
| Attention span | Long; they read everything and compare it with the last submission |
| Opening | The scope and the conclusion: compliant, gaps, remediation dates |
| Evidence they trust | Documents, logs, signed records, dates, named owners |
| Depth | Complete; the appendix is the deck |
| Tone | Formal, factual, no adjectives, no promise without a date |
| Switch off at | Marketing claims, vague ownership, undated plans |
| Ask they can grant | Acceptance, a closed finding, an agreed remediation date |

Structure: What, So what, Now what; a findings table (finding, status, owner, date) on one slide.

### Mixed audiences: how to layer

A steering group with engineers in it, a customer meeting with their CFO and their architect, an all-hands with the board dialling in. Layer the deck:

| Layer | Who reads it | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Headlines and the closing | Everyone; executives read only this | The `##` lines, the first and the last slide |
| Bullets and one chart per slide | Practitioners | The slide body |
| Detail, method, source data | Specialists | An appendix section after the closing, and `Notes:` |

Write the headlines for the most senior reader and the bullets for the most expert one. Put the ask where both see it: the first content slide and the last.

## Goals

| Goal | Structure (narratives.md) | Evidence | Where the ask goes | What to cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Decide | Pyramid; Options and a recommendation | One number per reason; the rest in the appendix | First content slide and the last slide | History, method, alternatives already rejected |
| Inform, report status | Chronological journey; What, So what, Now what | A status table, one chart | Last slide, as next steps with dates | Anything unchanged since the last report |
| Persuade, sell | Problem, Agitate, Solve; Hero's journey | Customer proof, a demo, a price | Last slide, one concrete step | Features, company history |
| Teach | Three acts per module | Worked examples, exercises | End of each block: the exercise | Edge cases, optional material |
| Inspire, align | Sparkline | Two or three proof points, one story | The call to adventure, two slides before the end | Every number except the memorable one; process |
| Warn: risk or incident | What, So what, Now what; SCQA | The timeline, the cost, the exposure | Right after the So what: the decision needed now | Blame, speculation, uncosted options |

## Formats

| Format | Words per slide | Font floor | Notes | Animation | Slides | Backup |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Live talk in a room | 25 or fewer | 24 pt | Full script in `Notes:` | None, or one build per slide | 1.5 minutes each | Appendix; a printed handout |
| Remote, webinar | 40 | 18 pt (laptop) | Full script | One build per slide, to hold attention | 1 minute each; something changes every 3 minutes | A PDF sent afterwards |
| Reading deck, sent by email | 80 to 120, full sentences | 16 pt body, 12 to 14 pt in tables | None; everything is on the slide | None | 8 to 15 | The data table as appendix slides |
| Pitch, time-boxed | 25 | 24 pt | Timed script, seconds per slide | None | 1 per minute | Appendix for questions: unit economics, team, competition |
| Workshop | 30, plus instruction slides | 24 pt | Facilitator notes: timing, what to collect | None | 5 per hour, plus exercises | Templates for the exercises, blank slides |
| Keynote | 10 or fewer | 32 pt | Full script, rehearsed | One build per slide at most | 0.5 to 1 minute each | None; the talk is the product |
| Demo | 15, mostly captions | 24 pt | Click-by-click script, fallback screenshots | None | One per scene | A screenshot of every scene, a recorded video |
| Lightning talk, 5 minutes | 15 | 28 pt | Script to the second | None | 5 to 10 | A link to the details |
| Board paper with slides | 60, full sentences | 14 pt | None | None | 5 to 8, as an appendix to the paper | The paper is the primary document |

Rules that hold across formats:

- The font floor is the smallest text anywhere on the slide, footnotes and axis labels included. Below the floor, cut content; do not shrink it.
- Animation means builds, never movement. One build per slide (reveal the second column, reveal the conclusion) is the ceiling.
- Speaker notes are the script in live formats and absent in reading formats. `storyboard_check.py` warns on missing notes; pass `--reading` for a reading deck.
- Backup slides go after the closing slide in an appendix section (`# Appendix`) so the closing stays the last slide shown.
- A deck that is both presented and sent needs two files: the talk version, and a reading version with the notes folded into the slides.

## Where the story starts: material types

**From data or analysis.** Follow data-to-story.md: inventory, profile, six lenses, rank, one slide per finding, then the Data story arc. The findings become the headlines; the full table goes to the appendix.

**From a long document or report.** Extract the argument, not the text:

1. List the document's headings. Each heading is a candidate section or slide.
2. Under each heading, find the one sentence the author would defend. That sentence, cut to 70 characters, is the headline.
3. Keep the numbers and the named examples; drop the prose.
4. Reorder for the audience: conclusions first for executives, even when the document builds up to them.
5. Cite the document on the closing slide and in `Source:` lines. The deck is a reading aid, not a replacement.

**From a project update.** Chronological journey: a status headline, done since last time, surprises, next milestones on a `timeline` slide, risks in a `table` (risk, status, owner, date), the ask. Start from the last update, not from kick-off.

**From a product.** Demo tour: who uses it and for what, then scenes in the order the user meets them, then what it costs and how to start. Every scene needs a screenshot in `images/` as the fallback.

**From a strategy.** Options and a recommendation, or Three horizons: the decision, the criteria, the comparison table, the recommendation, what it gives up, the ask. Ask the user for the options that were rejected; the audience will ask.

**From nothing.** Run the eight-question interview in narratives.md, then choose the structure from the mapping table there.

## Culture and language

Many audiences read the deck in their second or third language, and some read right to left (set `language:` in the front matter for Hebrew, Arabic, Persian or Urdu; the build mirrors the layout). Keep sentences under fifteen words. Write numbers with units and the base ("4.2 M revenue, of 36 M total"), never "4.2" alone. Avoid idioms, sports metaphors and puns; they cost the reader a second and sometimes the meaning. Spell out abbreviations on first use, even common ones.

Business cultures differ in how directly an ask is stated. In some rooms the expected form is "Approve X by date" on the first slide. In others the same request lands better as a recommendation the group discusses, with the decision recorded after the meeting, worded as "We propose X and welcome the group's view before 30 September". Ask the user which form their room expects; the structure does not change, only the wording of the ask slide and how early the recommendation appears.

Titles and hierarchy weigh more in some organisations. When the user says the room is formal, name the decision owner on the ask slide, keep humour out, and put the summary of the three messages before the ask rather than after it.

## Checklist before writing the storyboard

Run this list and record the answers at the top of the storyboard as a comment (`<!-- ... -->`):

1. Audience named: one profile from this file, plus the name of the person who decides.
2. Goal stated in one sentence: "At the end, the CFO approves two hires for Q4."
3. Format and time known: room, remote or reading; the minutes; whether it is read in advance.
4. The ask written as a headline, under 70 characters, with a date.
5. The three messages written, one line each; they become the closing slide.
6. Structure chosen from narratives.md and named in the comment.
7. Evidence listed: which numbers, which source, which chart for each message.

If any item is blank, ask the user for it before writing the storyboard.
